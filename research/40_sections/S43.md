## 43. Assumptions

Every assumption the design rests on is listed here, numbered, with its basis and with the specific fact that would falsify it. Nothing in this blueprint depends on an unstated assumption.

Identifier scheme: `ASM-nn`, authored in this section, in the same manner as `RISK-nn` is authored in §42. An assumption is not a finding. Where an assumption is also a client decision, it carries a forward reference to the open question in §44 that asks for a ruling, and to the question in §45 that puts it to Atha Group.

Reading the table: **Assumption** is what the build must take as true in the absence of a contrary instruction. **Falsified by** is the observable fact that would make it untrue. **Effect if falsified** is what has to change — this is the column that tells a coding agent how much of the build is at risk.

### 43.4 Two flagged design assumptions carried from the frozen formula registry

These two are separated out because they are not simple true/false premises. Each records a place where an authoritative external definition differs from the client's definition, and where this blueprint has taken a deliberate, disclosed position rather than silently adopting one.

**ASM-37 — Commitment relief has two gates in SAP, but one in this design.**

SAP Project System relieves commitment through two independent mechanisms. The goods receipt indicator on the purchase order item determines whether commitment reduces at goods receipt or at invoice receipt. The goods-receipt-non-valuated flag independently overrides this: when it is set, invoice information is used to reduce commitment. The dangerous combination is the goods receipt indicator set together with goods-receipt-non-valuated, because commitment is relieved at receipt while actual cost arrives only at invoice — leaving the item counted in neither actual nor commitment, and understating assigned value.

*Assumption taken:* our commitment ledger relieves commitment **only on the event that also creates actual cost** — the vendor bill — and carries an explicit received-not-billed exposure bucket so that nothing falls between the two. This is the anti-under-count control, and it is the exact mirror of the anti-double-count control.

*Basis:* the frozen formula registry ruling; the second gate is supported by tier-2 evidence only, and is recorded as such. The client document independently supports the single-gate outcome by stating that commitment remains for the unbilled PO balance at receipt.

*Falsified by:* Atha Group requiring commitment relief at goods receipt, or an audit ruling that received-not-billed value must be presented as actual cost rather than as a distinct exposure bucket.

*Effect if falsified:* the exposure model in §22 gains a third relief event, the received-not-billed bucket loses its purpose, and the anti-under-count control is removed. Both the client's section 8 arithmetic and the roll-forward reconciliation in §27 change.

**ASM-38 — The client's availability formula matches SAP's compact definition of assigned value, not its authoritative one.**

Two non-equivalent SAP definitions of assigned value exist, and they differ materially. The compact definition, given in the budgeting value display, is the total of commitment and actual postings, shown only when availability control is active `[source: https://help.sap.com/doc/acd8b65334e6b54ce10000000a174cb4/3.6/en-US/0213d553088f4308e10000000a174cb4.html — verified 2026-08-05]` (SAP Project System documentation; variant: S/4HANA on-premise documentation set). The authoritative definition, given on the assigned-values page, is a nine-component calculated aggregate — not a single stored field — which additionally includes statistical actual costs, settlement debits, settlement credits to budget-controlled objects, statistical actual costs of subordinate WBS elements that do not carry budget, and the **maximum** of plan versus actual-plus-commitment for assigned networks and orders `[source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/4dd8cb7b1c484b4b93af84d00f60fdb8/f711d553088f4308e10000000a174cb4.html — verified 2026-08-05]` (variant: S/4HANA on-premise).

Atha Group's formula — Available Budget = Current Approved Budget − Actual CWIP − Open PO Commitments — resembles the compact definition. Building availability control on it will therefore **systematically understate consumption and permit overspend relative to SAP behaviour**, in any situation where the additional components carry value.

*Assumption taken:* implement the client formula as the primary control exactly as stated, and expose the additional components as an explicit, separately reported "other assigned value" line, so that the understatement is visible rather than silent.

*Basis:* the frozen formula registry ruling. The client formula is binding; the disclosure is the blueprint's addition.

*Falsified by:* Atha Group ratifying the compact definition as sufficient for their control environment (in which case the additional line becomes informational only), or requiring the broader definition (in which case the primary control changes).

*Effect if falsified in the broader direction:* the availability calculation in §22 gains additional components, the client's own worked examples cease to reproduce, and the whole control model must be re-baselined with the client.

*Ruling required:* this is put to Atha Group as OQ-21 in §44 and as a question in §45. Until it is ratified, the primary control stays as the client stated it.

**ASM-39 — CWIP settlement has two destinations, not one.**

With no capitalisation key, the system settles all debits **not assigned to controlling receivers** to the asset under construction during periodic settlement. The qualifier matters: dropping it and asserting that 100 percent of debits go to the asset under construction would model CWIP as a total-cost sink and erase the non-capitalisable and pre-operative-expense write-off path to cost centres.

*Assumption taken:* the settlement design models **both** paths — capitalisable debits to the CWIP balance and onward to fixed assets, and debits routed to controlling receivers by a prior rule which never reach the CWIP balance at all.

*Basis:* the frozen formula registry ruling, with the qualifier restored. Settlement to an asset under construction is carried out using capitalization keys, which allow different percentage rates per depreciation area `[source: https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_me60/6.0.4/en-US/50/a1c6535e601e4be10000000a174cb4/content.htm?no_cache=true — verified 2026-08-05]` (variant: classic on-premise documentation set).

*Falsified by:* Atha Group's accounting policy treating all project debits as capitalisable, so that the second path is never used.

*Effect if falsified:* the capitalisation rule model in §23 simplifies to a single destination; the Pre-operative expenses and Contingency budget heads lose their distinct treatment — which is precisely the treatment OQ-14 asks the client and their auditor to confirm.
