### 3.2 The core control principle

The document states one governing formula, in section 1, and applies it throughout (REQ-BUD-001):

> Available Budget = Current Approved Budget − Actual CWIP − Open PO Commitments

Section 9 states how the left-hand budget figure is maintained (REQ-REV-006):

> Original Budget + Approved Revisions = Current Approved Budget

Two derived measures are used by the document but never written down as formulas in it. Section 8 uses a column headed "Budget Exposure"; section 12 uses a column headed "Utilisation / Exposure" and sets alert thresholds expressed as "budget exposure". Both are consistent at every printed row with:

> Exposure = Open PO Commitment + Actual CWIP
> Utilisation % = Exposure ÷ Current Approved Budget

These two definitions are **inferred from the client's own data, not asserted by the client's text**. All 13 numeric rows across the client document's three numeric tables satisfy them; no row fails; every column total in section 6 foots. See §44 (OQ-18) for the naming question this leaves open.

### 3.4 What the client document asks for, in its own terms

**Objectives (section 2, eight bullets).** Create a unique CAPEX/CWIP project code for every approved capital project; maintain project-level and budget-head-level approved budgets; validate budget availability before Purchase Order approval; treat approved/open Purchase Orders as committed expenditure; convert commitments into actual CWIP as vendor bills are booked without double counting; provide controlled budget revision and exceptional approval for overruns; maintain an audit trail from budget approval through procurement, accounting and final capitalization; provide management dashboards for Budget versus Commitment versus Actual versus Available Budget.

**Lifecycle (section 3, ten steps).** CAPEX Project Creation → Budget Approval → Purchase Request → Budget Availability Check → PR / Exception Approval → Purchase Order → Purchase Receive / GRN → Vendor Bill / CWIP → Project Completion → Asset Capitalization. Section 4 restates the same chain as a strictly linear diagram, asserting two equivalences: Purchase Order = Commitment, and Vendor Bill = Actual CWIP.

Three lifecycle rules in this sequence are load-bearing and must be implemented exactly as stated:

| Step | Rule as stated by the client | Consequence for the build |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Approved PO reserves/commits the budget | Commitment arises at PO, not at PR (see §44 OQ-10 and OQ-20) |
| 7 | Goods receipt does **not** release commitment; commitment remains for the unbilled PO balance | Receipt is not a commitment-relieving event. This is the anti-under-count control. |
| 8 | Vendor bill posts to the relevant CWIP GL and becomes actual project expenditure | Billing, and only billing, converts commitment to actual |

**Project master (section 5, ten fields).** CAPEX/CWIP Code (illustrative value CAPEX-2026-001), Project Name, Company/Unit, Plant/Location, Department, Project Owner, Original Budget, Current Approved Budget, CWIP GL (illustrative value "CWIP - Plant & Machinery"), and Status with the four values Active / Completed / Capitalized / Closed. Workflow step 1 additionally names Asset Category and Timeline, which do not appear in the section 5 field table.

**Budget revision (section 9, six stages).** Budget Exception Raised → Revision Request with mandatory justification → Approval by appropriate authority → Budget Version Update in which the original budget remains unchanged and the approved revision is separately recorded → Current Budget recomputed → PO Revalidation, in which the blocked transaction is rechecked against the revised available budget. Approval of a revision does not by itself release the transaction.

**Key controls (section 13, eight bullets).** Mandatory CAPEX Code and Budget Head on applicable capital procurement; no silent overwrite of the original approved budget; every revision retains requestor, approver, date, reason and approval reference; PO amendments trigger revalidation **for the incremental value**; PO cancellation or closure releases unused commitment; bill posting reduces open commitment and increases actual CWIP; closed or capitalized projects prevent further procurement unless reopened through approval; dashboard drill-down from project to PR, PO, receipt/GRN and vendor bill.

**Recommended alerts (lead-in above section 13, seven alerts).** 80% budget exposure; 90% budget exposure; budget exceeded; open PO ageing; pending GRN/billing; overdue CWIP projects; projects awaiting capitalization. The client gives thresholds for the first two only. Recipients, channel, frequency and evaluation level are not stated for any of the seven.

**The client's own solution mapping (section 10, thirteen rows).** This is the client's own view of build effort, expressed as product/effort labels — not as statements about API availability.

The client's own table therefore already concedes that the five components at the heart of this engagement — budget master, commitment register, availability validation, revision control and exception routing — are custom build, not Zoho ERP standard functionality. That concession is the strongest client-side support for the architecture recommended in §12.

**Architecture (section 11, three layers).** Zoho ERP (PR → Approval → PO → Purchase Receive/GRN → Vendor Bill → CWIP Accounting), connected bidirectionally by "API / Workflow / Custom Function" to a CAPEX Budget Control Layer (Project Master → Budget Heads → Commitment Register → Budget Validation → Revision / Exception Approval), feeding Zoho Analytics (Budget vs Commitment vs Actual vs Available, with Project / Plant / Department / Budget Head drill-down).

**Implementation approach (section 14, six phases).** Discovery & Design; ERP Configuration; Budget Control Build; Integration & Automation; Analytics; UAT & Go-Live. The client's own Phase 1 states what must be confirmed before build: the Atha Group CAPEX approval matrix, project structure, budget heads, entities/plants and accounting policy. That list is the client's own acknowledgement that these five things are not defined anywhere in the document.

### 3.6 The client document contains no WBS hierarchy — this is stated explicitly

The client document defines exactly two structural levels: the **CAPEX/CWIP project code** (section 5) and the **budget head** (section 6). The deepest structural level present anywhere in the document is the budget-head row of the section 6 table. The only statement of budget granularity in the entire document is the section 2 bullet: *maintain project-level and budget-head-level approved budgets*.

An exhaustive search of the extracted document text confirms that the terms "WBS", "work breakdown", "hierarchy", "level", "parent" and "child" do not appear in any structural sense anywhere in it. The section 5 project field list contains no hierarchy field, no parent field and no level field.

Therefore:

- **Multi-level WBS is an engagement-brief requirement, not a client requirement.** It is carried throughout this blueprint as an explicitly labelled scope addition. No WBS requirement appears in the client requirement register, and none may be presented to Atha Group as something they asked for.
- **The data model makes WBS optional depth.** A project with zero WBS elements must behave exactly as the client document describes: budget and availability control at project plus budget head only, producing exactly the section 6 table. A WBS-enabled project must roll every WBS-level figure up to the same project plus budget-head totals that the section 6 table shows. The client's control model is the floor; WBS is an optional layer above it that never changes the floor's arithmetic.
- **Every WBS-only row in the traceability matrix (§4) carries the source marker `MASTER PROMPT - NOT IN CLIENT PDF`.**
- **The decision is Atha Group's, and it is asked first in §45.** Does Atha Group want budget control below budget-head level; if so, how many levels; and who owns WBS coding.
